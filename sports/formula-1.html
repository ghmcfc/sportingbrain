Sport guide

Formula 1

The fastest racing on earth, decided as much in the garage and the pit lane as on track.

Photo: The grid at Silverstone. ToNG!? / Wikimedia Commons

How it worksWhat to watch forThe greatsTalk like an expertWhen it's onGet involved

How it works

Twenty drivers in ten teams race on circuits around the world. Qualifying on Saturday decides the starting grid; the race is usually about 305km.

Every car must use at least two different tyre compounds in a dry race, so every driver stops at least once. Pit strategy, choosing when to stop and on which tyres, is often where races are won.

Teams build their own cars within tight technical rules, and a budget cap limits spending. The car matters enormously, which is why the battle between teammates is the clearest measure of a driver.

Scoring

Points go to the top ten: 25, 18, 15, 12, 10, 8, 6, 4, 2, 1. Some weekends also have a shorter sprint race with points for the top eight.

Formats

  • Grand Prix weekends: practice, qualifying, race.
  • Sprint weekends: a short Saturday race as well as the Sunday Grand Prix.

What the experts watch for

  1. The undercut: pit first, use fresh tyres to go faster, and come out ahead when your rival stops.
  2. Tyre degradation: watch lap times creep up as the tyres wear out.
  3. Track position: on circuits where overtaking is hard, qualifying is almost everything.
  4. Safety cars: a cheap pit stop under the safety car can turn a race.

The greats

Lewis Hamilton

Seven world titles and the record for wins and pole positions.

Michael Schumacher

Seven world titles, five in a row with Ferrari.

Ayrton Senna

Three world titles and a reputation as the greatest qualifier of all.

Max Verstappen

Four world titles in a row from 2021 to 2024.

Lella Lombardi

The only woman to score World Championship points, in 1975.

Talk like an expert

Tap a term to see what it means.

DRS

A flap on the rear wing that opens to help an attacking car overtake.

Undercut

Pitting before your rival to gain time on fresh tyres.

Parc fermé

Rules that stop teams changing the car between qualifying and the race.

Dirty air

Turbulent air behind a car that makes following closely hard.

Box, box

Radio call telling a driver to come into the pits.

When it's on
  • March to December: The championship season, around 24 races.
