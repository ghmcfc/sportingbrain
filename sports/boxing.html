Sport guide

Boxing

The hardest sport to get good at, and the easiest to understand.

How it worksWhat to watch forThe greatsTalk like an expertWhen it's onGet involved

How it works

Two boxers fight over a set number of rounds, usually three minutes each for men and two for women in professional boxing. They can only punch with closed fists, above the belt.

A fight ends by knockout, stoppage (the referee or a corner decides a boxer can't continue), disqualification, or on the judges' scorecards.

Fighters compete in weight classes, from minimumweight to heavyweight. Each class has world champions from four main bodies: WBA, WBC, IBF and WBO. Holding all four makes you undisputed.

Scoring

Three judges score each round, usually 10-9 to the boxer who won it. A knockdown usually makes it 10-8.

Formats

  • Professional: up to 12 rounds for title fights.
  • Amateur and Olympic: three rounds, scored differently.

What the experts watch for

  1. The jab: the most important punch in boxing, used to control distance.
  2. Body shots: they don't look dramatic but they slow a fighter down in the later rounds.
  3. Ring generalship: who is controlling where the fight happens.

The greats

Muhammad Ali

Three-time heavyweight champion.

Sugar Ray Robinson

Widely rated the best pound-for-pound fighter ever.

Claressa Shields

Two Olympic golds and undisputed titles in more than one weight class.

Katie Taylor

Undisputed lightweight champion and Olympic gold medallist in 2012.

Joe Louis

Heavyweight champion for nearly twelve years.

Talk like an expert

Tap a term to see what it means.

Southpaw

A boxer who leads with the right hand and right foot.

Pound for pound

Rankings of the best boxers regardless of weight.

Clinch

Holding to stop the opponent punching.

Undisputed

Holding all four major world titles in a weight class.

TKO

Technical knockout: the referee stops the fight.

When it's on
  • All year: Big fights are made by promoters, not to a fixed calendar.
  • Every four years: Olympic boxing.
