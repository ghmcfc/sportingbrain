Golf

The fewest shots wins, and the hardest opponent is the course.

Photo: The Old Course, St Andrews. Optograph / Wikimedia Commons

How it worksWhat to watch forThe greatsTalk like an expertWhen it's onGet involved

How it works

Players play 18 holes, each with a par: the number of shots a good player should take. The aim is to complete the round in as few strokes as possible.

Professional tournaments usually last four rounds over four days, with a cut after two rounds that halves the field.

The course changes everything: wind on a links course, firm greens in a US Open, the slopes at Augusta. Strategy is about where to miss as much as where to aim.

Scoring

Stroke play: total strokes over the event. Match play: each hole is won, lost or halved, and you win the match by winning more holes.

Formats

  • Stroke play: most professional events.
  • Match play: the Ryder Cup and Solheim Cup.
  • Team events: Ryder Cup (men, Europe v USA) and Solheim Cup (women).

What the experts watch for

  1. Strokes gained: shows whether a player is winning shots with driving, approach play, short game or putting.
  2. Pin positions: a Sunday flag tucked by water changes who attacks and who plays safe.
  3. The leaderboard pressure: final-round leaders often play the back nine very differently from the front.

The greats

Jack Nicklaus

18 men's majors.

Tiger Woods

15 men's majors, including the "Tiger Slam" of four in a row in 2000-01.

Annika Sörenstam

10 women's majors and the first woman to shoot 59 in an LPGA event.

Patty Berg

15 women's majors, the record.

Seve Ballesteros

Five majors and the soul of the modern Ryder Cup.

Talk like an expert

Albatross

Three under par on a hole.

Up and down

Getting into the hole in two shots from off the green.

Links

A coastal course on sandy ground, like St Andrews.

Fourball

Pairs, each player plays their own ball, best score counts.

Foursomes

Pairs take alternate shots with one ball.

When it's on
  • April: The Masters.
  • May to July: PGA Championship, US Open, The Open.
  • Through the year: Five women's majors.
  • Every two years: Ryder Cup (men) and Solheim Cup (women).
