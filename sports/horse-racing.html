Horse racing

Speed, stamina and form, on the Flat and over jumps.

Photo: Aintree Racecourse. Rodhullandemu / Wikimedia Commons

How it works

Flat racing is run without obstacles over distances from five furlongs to over two miles. Jump racing (National Hunt) is run over hurdles or bigger, solid fences called chases.

Handicaps give better horses more weight to carry, to even out the field. Weight-for-age races adjust for younger horses.

Form is everything to racing fans: previous runs, the going (how firm or soft the ground is), the distance and the trainer.

Scoring

First past the post wins, subject to the stewards.

Formats

  • Flat: the Classics, such as the Derby and the 2,000 Guineas.
  • Jumps: hurdles and chases, including the Cheltenham Gold Cup and Grand National.

What the experts watch for

  1. The going: some horses only run well on soft ground, some only on fast.
  2. The pace: a slow early pace suits horses with speed at the finish.
  3. Jumping: in National Hunt, a bad jump at the second-last can lose a race.
