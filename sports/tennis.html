Tennis

A one-on-one fight where the scoring system is designed to produce drama.

Photo: Centre Court, Wimbledon. GATORFAN2525 / Wikimedia Commons

How it works

Players hit the ball over the net into the opponent's court. The ball may bounce once. Serve goes diagonally into the service box, and you get two attempts.

Scoring is nested: points make games, games make sets, sets make matches. You must win a game by two points and a set by two games, until a tie-break settles it at 6-6.

Surfaces matter. Clay is slow and high-bouncing and rewards patience and spin. Grass is fast and low and rewards serving and short points. Hard courts sit in between.

Scoring

Points go 15, 30, 40, game. At 40-40 (deuce) you need two clear points. Sets are first to six games with a two-game lead, or a tie-break at 6-6.

Formats

  • Best of three sets: most tour events.
  • Best of five sets: men's singles at the four Grand Slams.
  • Doubles and mixed doubles.

What the experts watch for

  1. First-serve percentage: below about 60 per cent, a big server is in trouble.
  2. Break points saved: tells you who is winning the pressure moments.
  3. Court position: who is standing on the baseline and who has been pushed back behind it.
  4. Second-serve return: aggressive returners stand inside the baseline and attack it.

Talk like an expert

Tap a term to see what it means.

Break of serve

Winning a game when your opponent is serving.

Bagel

Winning a set 6-0.

Golden set

Winning a set without dropping a single point. Very rare.

Hawk-Eye

Ball-tracking system used for line calls.

Moonball

A deliberately high, looping shot to push an opponent back.
