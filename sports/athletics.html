Sport guide

Athletics

Run, jump, throw. The simplest sport there is, measured to the hundredth.

Photo: Running track, west London. David Hawgood / Wikimedia Commons

How it works

Athletics covers track running (from 100m to 10,000m, plus hurdles, steeplechase and relays), field events (jumps and throws), road running such as the marathon, race walking, and the combined events.

Sprints and hurdles are run in lanes. From 800m upwards athletes break from their lanes, and tactics take over: who leads, who sits in, when to kick.

In the field events athletes get a set number of attempts and their best counts. In the high jump and pole vault they choose the heights they attempt, and three failures at a height ends their competition.

Scoring

First across the line wins a race (measured by the torso). In field events the longest or highest valid attempt wins. The heptathlon and decathlon use points tables.

Formats

  • Track and field meetings, from local leagues to the Diamond League.
  • Road races and marathons.
  • Cross country.
  • Combined events: women's heptathlon (seven events), men's decathlon (ten).

What the experts watch for

  1. The bell lap in middle distance: who has the gears for the final 200m?
  2. Reaction times in the 100m: under 0.1 seconds is a false start.
  3. Splits in distance races: an even pace usually means a fast time; a slow early pace means a sprint finish.
  4. Takeoff boards in the long jump: a centimetre over and the jump is a foul.

The greats

Usain Bolt

Eight Olympic golds and the 100m and 200m world records.

Fanny Blankers-Koen

Four golds at the 1948 Olympics, aged 30, with two children at home.

Jackie Joyner-Kersee

Two Olympic heptathlon golds and the world record.

Faith Kipyegon

Three Olympic 1500m titles in a row (2016, 2020, 2024).

Mondo Duplantis

Has broken the pole vault world record many times since 2020.

Jesse Owens

Four golds at the 1936 Berlin Olympics.

Talk like an expert

Tap a term to see what it means.

Kick

The final burst of speed in a distance race.

PB

Personal best.

Pacemaker

A runner paid to set the pace who usually drops out.

Wind-assisted

A sprint or jump with a tailwind over 2m/s. It can't count as a record.

Fosbury Flop

The backwards high-jump technique introduced by Dick Fosbury in 1968.

When it's on
  • Every four years: The Olympics.
  • Every two years: World Championships.
  • Summer: The Diamond League circuit.
  • Spring and autumn: The big city marathons.
