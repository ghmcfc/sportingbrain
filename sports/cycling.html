Sport guide

Cycling

An individual sport that is won by teams, and decided by the wind and the mountains.

Photo: Track racing at the Parc de la Tête d'Or velodrome. Jejecam / Wikimedia Commons

How it works

Road racing is the best-known form: one-day races and multi-day stage races such as the Tour de France, Giro d'Italia and Vuelta a España.

Riding in another rider's slipstream saves a lot of energy, so teams work together to protect their leader, chase down breakaways and set up sprints.

In a stage race the overall winner is the rider with the lowest total time. Most of the time gaps are made in the mountains and the time trials.

Scoring

Stage races: lowest total time wins the yellow jersey. There are also points (green) and mountains (polka dot) classifications.

Formats

  • Grand Tours: three weeks.
  • One-day Classics, such as Paris-Roubaix.
  • Track cycling: sprint, keirin, team pursuit, omnium, madison.
  • BMX and mountain biking.

What the experts watch for

  1. The breakaway: a small group goes clear early. Does the peloton let them go?
  2. Echelons: crosswinds split the bunch into diagonal lines, and anyone at the back loses time.
  3. Lead-out trains: teams line up riders to deliver their sprinter at full speed in the final 200m.

The greats

Eddy Merckx

Five Tours, five Giros, and the most wins in the sport's history.

Marianne Vos

World champion in road, cyclo-cross and track.

Annemiek van Vleuten

Won the first modern Tour de France Femmes in 2022.

Laura Kenny

Five Olympic track golds for Great Britain.

Jason Kenny

Seven Olympic golds, a British record.
