Squash

Two players in a glass box, and the fittest usually wins.

How it worksWhat to watch forThe greatsTalk like an expertWhen it's onGet involved

How it works

Two players take turns hitting the ball against the front wall of an enclosed court. The ball can bounce once on the floor and use the side and back walls.

Getting to the T, the centre of the court, is the key. From there you can reach anything; your opponent is forced into the corners.

Players must give each other room to play their shot. If they don't, the referee awards a let (replay) or a stroke (point).

Scoring

Professional matches use point-a-rally scoring to 11, best of five games, winning by two.

Formats

  • Singles and doubles.

What the experts watch for

  1. Length: tight drives down the walls push the opponent to the back.
  2. The drop shot: a soft shot into the front corner.
  3. Movement: the best players glide to the ball and back to the T.

The greats

Jahangir Khan

555 matches unbeaten from 1981 to 1986.

Heather McKay

16 British Open titles in a row.

Nour El Sherbini

Several women's world titles for Egypt.

Jansher Khan

Eight world titles.

Talk like an expert

The T

The centre of the court.

Let

A replayed point.

Stroke

A point awarded after interference.

Boast

A shot off the side wall first.

Nick

The join between wall and floor. A ball that hits it dies.

When it's on
  • Year-round: PSA World Tour.
  • Every year: World Championships and the British Open.
