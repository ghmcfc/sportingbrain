Rugby union

Fifteen-a-side, and much of it is a fight for the ball you can barely see.

Photo: Twickenham Stadium. Diliff / Wikimedia Commons

How it worksWhat to watch forThe greatsTalk like an expertWhen it's onGet involved

How it works

Two teams of 15 try to carry, pass or kick the ball over the opponents' try line. Passes must go sideways or backwards. Knock the ball forwards and play stops for a scrum.

When a player is tackled they must release the ball, and players from both sides compete for it in a ruck. Most penalties come from this contest: going off your feet, coming in from the side, or not releasing.

Set pieces restart the game: scrums after knock-ons and forward passes, lineouts when the ball goes out of play. A dominant scrum or lineout can win a match on its own.

Scoring

Try 5 points. Conversion 2. Penalty goal 3. Drop goal 3.

Formats

  • Fifteens: 80 minutes, two halves.
  • Sevens: seven-a-side, seven-minute halves, and an Olympic sport since 2016.

What the experts watch for

  1. The breakdown: count how many players each side commits to a ruck. Too many and the defence is short; too few and the ball is stolen.
  2. The gain line: is the ball carrier going forwards when tackled? Teams that win the gain line usually win the game.
  3. The kicking battle: box kicks from the scrum-half and long touch-finders are about territory, not possession.
  4. The bench: replacements around the 50th minute, especially in the front row, often decide the last half hour.

The greats

Jonah Lomu

15 World Cup tries for New Zealand.

Richie McCaw

Captained New Zealand to the 2011 and 2015 World Cups.

Jonny Wilkinson

The drop goal that won the 2003 World Cup.

Portia Woodman-Wickliffe

Record-breaking New Zealand try scorer in fifteens and sevens.

Emily Scarratt

England centre, 2014 World Cup winner and World Rugby Player of the Year in 2019.

Gareth Edwards

Wales scrum-half, scorer of "that try" for the Barbarians in 1973.

Talk like an expert

Tap a term to see what it means.

Jackal

A defender who gets over the ball at a ruck to steal it.

Bomb squad

A heavy bench of forwards brought on to dominate late.

Crossing

Obstructing a defender with a decoy runner. A penalty.

Pod

A group of forwards set up to take the ball up together.

Advantage

Play continues after an offence while the other side gains from it.

When it's on
  • February and March: Men's and women's Six Nations.
  • Every four years: Men's Rugby World Cup (2027, Australia).
  • Every few years: Women's Rugby World Cup (England won in 2025).
