Football

Eleven against eleven, and the cleverest game is the one you don't see on the highlights.

Photo: Wembley Stadium, London. Jbmg40 / Wikimedia Commons

How it works

Two teams of eleven, 90 minutes split into two halves, and the only way to win is to put the ball in the net more often than the other lot. Only the goalkeeper can use their hands, and only inside their own penalty area.

The offside law is what shapes everything. An attacker is offside if, when the ball is played to them, they are in the opponent's half and nearer the goal line than both the ball and the second-last defender. It is why defences step up together, why strikers run along the line, and why a centimetre on a VAR replay can cancel a goal.

Most of the game is played without the ball. Elite teams now measure how quickly they win it back (pressing), how high their defensive line sits, and how many passes they let the opponent make before they engage.

Scoring

A goal counts when the whole ball crosses the whole goal line between the posts and under the bar. In league football a win is three points and a draw one.

Formats

  • League: everyone plays everyone home and away; most points wins.
  • Cup: knockout, with extra time and penalties if level.
  • Tournaments such as the World Cup: groups, then knockouts.

What the experts watch for

  1. The defensive line: watch how high it sits and whether it moves up together. That is where offside traps and balls in behind come from.
  2. Pressing triggers: a back pass, a bad touch or a pass to the touchline is often the signal for a team to swarm.
  3. The full-backs: in many modern systems one tucks into midfield and the other pushes high, changing the team's shape with and without the ball.
  4. Set pieces: about a quarter to a third of goals in top leagues come from dead balls, and clubs now employ specialist coaches for them.

The greats

Pelé

Three World Cups with Brazil (1958, 1962, 1970).

Diego Maradona

Dragged Argentina to the 1986 World Cup and Napoli to two Serie A titles.

Lionel Messi

Record eight Ballon d'Or wins and the 2022 World Cup.

Marta

The all-time top scorer at Women's World Cups, with 17 goals.

Birgit Prinz

Two World Cups and three-time FIFA World Player of the Year with Germany.

Alexia Putellas

Back-to-back Ballon d'Or Féminin winner, 2021 and 2022.

Talk like an expert

Tap a term to see what it means.

Low block

A team defending deep, close to its own goal, with little space between the lines.

Inverted full-back

A full-back who moves into central midfield when their team has the ball.

Expected goals (xG)

The probability that a shot is scored, based on thousands of similar shots.

Half-space

The channel between the centre and the wing, where creative players like to receive the ball.

Rest defence

The players a team keeps back while it attacks, ready to stop a counter.

When it's on
  • August to May: Domestic league seasons in England and most of Europe.
  • Every four years: Men's World Cup (2026, 2030) and Women's World Cup (2027, 2031).
  • Late May / early June: Champions League finals, men's and women's.
