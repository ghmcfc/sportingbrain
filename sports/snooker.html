Snooker

Geometry under pressure, and a 147 is the perfect frame.

Photo: The Crucible, Sheffield. Chris Downer / Wikimedia Commons

How it worksWhat to watch forThe greatsTalk like an expertWhen it's onGet involved

How it works

Players pot 15 reds and six colours. Pot a red, then a colour, then a red, and so on. The colours come back onto the table until the reds are gone, then they are potted in order from yellow to black.

The real game is position: where the cue ball finishes after each shot. A good break is planned several shots ahead.

When you can't pot, you play safe, leaving the opponent without a shot, or snooker them so they can't hit the ball they need to.

Scoring

Red 1, yellow 2, green 3, brown 4, blue 5, pink 6, black 7. Fouls give your opponent at least four points.

Formats

  • Frames: matches are best-of a set number of frames. The World Championship final is best of 35.

What the experts watch for

  1. Safety exchanges: long, tense battles where nobody wants to give the other a chance.
  2. Break-building: does the player keep the cue ball in the middle of the table?
  3. Snookers required: when a player is behind by more than is left on the table, they need the opponent to give away fouls.

The greats

Ronnie O'Sullivan

Seven world titles and the fastest 147 ever made.

Stephen Hendry

Seven world titles in the 1990s.

Steve Davis

Six world titles in the 1980s.

Reanne Evans

Twelve women's world titles.

Joe Davis

Won the first 15 World Championships, 1927 to 1946.

Talk like an expert

Tap a term to see what it means.

Century

A break of 100 or more.

Plant

Hitting one ball onto another to pot it.

Free ball

After a foul leaves you snookered, you can nominate any ball as a red.

Re-spotted black

The decider used when scores are level at the end of a frame.

Long pot

A pot from distance, usually the length of the table.

When it's on
  • April to May: World Championship at the Crucible, Sheffield.
  • November to January: UK Championship and the Masters.
