Swimming

Four strokes, eight lanes and a lot of hundredths.

Photo: London Aquatics Centre. OlafJanssen / Wikimedia Commons

How it works

Races are swum in four strokes: freestyle (in practice front crawl), backstroke, breaststroke and butterfly, plus individual medley (all four) and relays.

Pools are 50m (long course) or 25m (short course). The start, the turns and the underwater phase after each wall make a big difference.

Swimmers are seeded through heats and semi-finals into an eight-lane final. The fastest swimmers go in the middle lanes.

Scoring

Fastest time wins.

Formats

  • Long course (50m) for the Olympics.
  • Short course (25m) championships.
  • Open water: 10km races.

What the experts watch for

  1. Splits: the time at each 50m tells you who went out too fast.
  2. Underwater kicking off the walls.
  3. The touch: races are often won by the length of a fingernail.

The greats

Michael Phelps

23 Olympic golds.

Katie Ledecky

Won the Olympic 800m freestyle at four Games in a row, 2012 to 2024.

Adam Peaty

Two Olympic 100m breaststroke golds.

Mark Spitz

Seven golds at the 1972 Olympics.
