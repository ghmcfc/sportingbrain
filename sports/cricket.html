Cricket

A game of patience, pressure and very precise maths, in three very different formats.

Photo: The Pavilion, Lord's. PaddyBriggs / Wikimedia Commons

How it works

Two teams of eleven take turns to bat and bowl. The batting side scores runs; the fielding side tries to take ten wickets to end the innings. Bowlers deliver six-ball overs from alternate ends.

There are several ways to be out, but most dismissals are bowled, caught, leg before wicket, run out or stumped. LBW, the one that causes most arguments, depends on where the ball pitched, where it hit the pad and whether it would have gone on to hit the stumps.

The ball is the hidden character. A new ball swings and bounces; an old one can reverse swing; a worn pitch helps spinners. Captains plan around all of it.

Scoring

Runs come from running between the wickets, or from boundaries: four if the ball reaches the rope after bouncing, six if it clears it. Extras such as wides and no-balls add to the total.

Formats

  • Test: up to five days, two innings each. The purest test of skill and stamina.
  • One-day international: 50 overs a side.
  • Twenty20: 20 overs a side, about three hours, and the format of the franchise leagues.
  • The Hundred: 100 balls a side, played in England and Wales.

What the experts watch for

  1. Field placings: a third man and a fine leg back mean the captain expects the edge; a short leg and silly point mean the spinner is attacking.
  2. Run rate versus required rate: in a chase, watch the gap. Once it climbs above about 10 an over in T20, sides usually start losing.
  3. The new ball: in Tests the first hour can decide the day.
  4. Bowling changes: who comes on, from which end and with which field, tells you what the captain is thinking.

The greats

Don Bradman

A Test batting average of 99.94, about 40 runs higher than anyone else with a long career.

Sachin Tendulkar

100 international centuries.

Muttiah Muralitharan

800 Test wickets.

Rachael Heyhoe Flint

Captained England to the first ever Cricket World Cup, in 1973.

Ellyse Perry

Australian all-rounder, also a football international.

Garfield Sobers

The first to hit six sixes in an over in first-class cricket, in 1968.

Talk like an expert

Tap a term to see what it means.

Reverse swing

An old ball swinging the opposite way to a new one, at pace.

Nightwatchman

A lower-order batter sent in late in the day to protect a better one.

Powerplay

Overs where only a few fielders may be outside the inner circle.

Doosra

An off-spinner's delivery that turns the other way.

Declaration

A captain ending their side's innings early to have time to bowl the opposition out.

When it's on
  • Every two years or so: Men's and women's T20 World Cups.
  • Every four years: The 50-over World Cups.
  • English summer: County Championship, the Hundred, and touring Test sides.
  • Regularly: The Ashes, England against Australia, for men and women.
