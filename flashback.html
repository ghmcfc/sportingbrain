Flashback

Not the ones you already know. The moments sport forgot, told properly, with the numbers and the sources.

Tennis · July 1887
Wimbledon champion at 15, in a corset

Lottie Dod won Wimbledon five times, then went off and won the British golf title, played hockey for England and took an Olympic medal in archery.

Cycling · August 1899
The fastest man on earth

Major Taylor was the world's best sprint cyclist when cycling was the biggest sport in America. He raced through threats, bans and attacks, and died in a charity ward.

Athletics · 30 August 1904
The worst Olympic marathon ever run

One well for water. A winner who arrived by car. A champion kept going on rat poison. St Louis, 1904.

Football · 26 December 1920
53,000 at Goodison, then a 50-year ban

The biggest crowd for a women's club match stood for almost a century. Within a year, the FA had shut women out of every ground it controlled.

Athletics · August 1948
"Too old to make the grade"

She was 30, a mother of two, and told by her critics to stay at home. Fanny Blankers-Koen went to London and won four gold medals.

Athletics · 29 May 1954
The other miracle mile

Everyone knows Bannister broke four minutes. Twenty-three days later, a chemist from Birmingham broke five, and the athletics authorities refused to count it.

Cricket · 28 July 1973
The first Cricket World Cup was a women's one

Two years before the men got round to it, England's women won a World Cup at Edgbaston, paid for by a millionaire and driven by a captain who would not take no for an answer.

Gymnastics · July 1976
The landing on a broken knee

Japan needed every point to beat the Soviet Union for team gold. Shun Fujimoto had broken his kneecap on the floor. He did not tell anyone.

Squash · 11 November 1986
555 and then Toulouse

For five and a half years nobody beat Jahangir Khan. Not once. Then a New Zealander who had lost to him 30 times walked on court in France.

Athletics · 7 August 1992
The 26th lap

An Ethiopian 20-year-old and a white South African ran the 10,000 metres together. Then they ran one more lap, hand in hand.
