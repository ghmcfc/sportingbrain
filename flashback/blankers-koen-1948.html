Flashback · Athletics

"Too old to make the grade"

She was 30, a mother of two, and told by her critics to stay at home. Fanny Blankers-Koen went to London and won four gold medals.

August 1948 ·

4gold medals at one Games, a first for a woman
30her age in London
2children at home
6world records she held going into the Games

The 1948 London Games were the first Olympics for twelve years. Fanny Blankers-Koen had been at the last ones, in Berlin in 1936, as an 18-year-old. In the years between she had married, had two children, and lived through the German occupation of the Netherlands.

By 1948 she held six world records at once. That did not stop people writing her off. Some journalists said 30 was too old for a woman athlete. Britain's team manager, Jack Crump, said she was "too old to make the grade". At home in the Netherlands, plenty thought she should be looking after her children instead.

The rules let athletes enter only three individual events. She left out the high jump and the long jump and focused on the track.

She won the 100 metres. She won the 80 metres hurdles. She won the 200 metres by a distance. Then she ran in the Dutch 4 x 100 metres relay and won that too. Four gold medals at one Games: no woman had done it before.

The press called her "the Flying Housewife". The name stuck, and so did the point she had made.

What happened next

She went home to a hero's welcome in Amsterdam, and her critics went quiet.
