The first Cricket World Cup was a women's one

Two years before the men got round to it, England's women won a World Cup at Edgbaston, paid for by a millionaire and driven by a captain who would not take no for an answer.

28 July 1973 ·

Ask most people when the first Cricket World Cup was held and they will say 1975. They are two years out.

The first one was in England in the summer of 1973, and it was for women. It happened because two people pushed for it: Rachael Heyhoe Flint, England's captain and the best-known woman in the game, and Sir Jack Hayward, a businessman who loved cricket. Hayward came up with the idea and put in £40,000 to pay for it.

Seven teams took part: England, Australia, New Zealand, Trinidad and Tobago, Jamaica, an International XI, and a Young England side. Matches were 60 overs a side. There was no knockout final. The tournament was a round-robin, and by luck of the fixture list the last match, England against Australia at Edgbaston on 28 July, decided it.

Enid Bakewell made 118. Heyhoe Flint added 64. England reached 279 for 3 from their 60 overs. Australia finished on 187 for 9, 92 runs short.

England won the tournament with 20 points to Australia's 17. Two years later, when the men held their first World Cup, they were following a trail the women had already made.

What happened next

England have won the Women's World Cup four times, most recently in 2017 at Lord's.
