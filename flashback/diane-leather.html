The other miracle mile

Everyone knows Bannister broke four minutes. Twenty-three days later, a chemist from Birmingham broke five, and the athletics authorities refused to count it.

29 May 1954 ·

4:59.6her mile, 29 May 1954
23days after Bannister
1967year the women's mile was recognised
1972first women's Olympic 1500m

On 6 May 1954 Roger Bannister ran the mile in 3 minutes 59.4 seconds at Iffley Road in Oxford. It became one of the most famous moments in British sport.

On 29 May 1954, at the Alexander Sports Ground in Birmingham, Diane Leather ran it in 4 minutes 59.6 seconds. She was the first woman to break five minutes.

Leather was an analytical chemist at the University of Birmingham who trained around her job, much as Bannister trained around medicine. Women's distance running was barely tolerated at the time. Women had not been allowed to run anything longer than 200 metres at the Olympics since 1928, when officials decided the 800 metres was too much for them.

Her run was a world best, but it could not be a world record. The governing body of athletics did not recognise the women's mile at all until 1967, so the record books had no place to put it.

She never got to run her best distances at an Olympics, because the women's 1500 metres did not appear until 1972.

Bannister's run is in every history of British sport. Hers, run in the same month, is in very few.

What happened next

The Westminster Mile now presents a trophy in her name.
