The landing on a broken knee

Japan needed every point to beat the Soviet Union for team gold. Shun Fujimoto had broken his kneecap on the floor. He did not tell anyone.

July 1976 ·

The men's team event at the 1976 Montreal Olympics was a two-horse race between Japan and the Soviet Union, and it was close all the way.

During the floor exercise, Shun Fujimoto badly injured his right knee. He had broken his kneecap. In a team event every routine counts, and if he withdrew, Japan would have to compete a man short with no margin for error. So he hid it and kept going.

On the pommel horse he scored 9.5. Then came the rings, which ends with a dismount from around eight feet up, straight down onto the legs.

He completed the routine and landed. The impact dislocated the broken kneecap and tore ligaments in his leg. He held the landing. The judges gave him 9.7.

Doctors told him to stop or risk being disabled for life. Japan won the team gold by a narrow margin over the Soviet Union, and Fujimoto's scores were part of that margin.

The story is often told with him collapsing after the dismount. That part is not true. He stayed on his feet.

What happened next

In 2017 he was inducted into the International Gymnastics Hall of Fame.
