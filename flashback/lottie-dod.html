Flashback · Tennis · 1887

Wimbledon champion at 15, in a corset

Lottie Dod won Wimbledon five times, then went off and won the British golf title, played hockey for England and took an Olympic medal in archery.

July 1887 ·

In 1887 Lottie Dod won the Wimbledon ladies' singles at 15 years and 285 days old. No one younger has ever won it. She played in the clothing of the time, including a corset of metal and whalebone that cut into her as she played.

Her tennis was decades ahead. She took the ball early, just before the top of the bounce, and hit flat, firm groundstrokes when most women patted the ball back. She served underarm and still dominated. She won Wimbledon in 1887, 1888, 1891, 1892 and 1893.

Then, at about 21, she stopped playing tennis. She had other things to do.

She rode the Cresta Run toboggan at St Moritz. She climbed mountains over 4,000 metres in the Alps. She played hockey for England twice against Ireland, scoring both England goals in the 1900 win. In 1904, at Troon, she won the British Ladies Amateur golf championship, the first woman to hold British titles at both tennis and golf.

And in 1908, at the London Olympics, she won silver in archery.

There are better tennis players in history. There may be no better all-round sportswoman.

What happened next

Nearly 140 years on, she is still the youngest ladies' singles champion in Wimbledon history.
